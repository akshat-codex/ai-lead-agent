You have roughly 72% of a real lead agent — but it is currently returning ~0% of its value, because every paid provider credential in backend/.env is commented out.

The reasoning machinery here is genuinely better than average: a deterministic hard-rule engine that an LLM structurally cannot override, an evidence-provenance model with real conflict handling, and a compound-ICP corroboration path that survives Explorium's OR-only search semantics. 02Any ICP with a custom rule can never reach PASSCritical

validate_against_icp builds its Candidate with custom_rule_results={} hardcoded, and nothing anywhere in the codebase ever populates that dict. The rule engine turns each unresolved custom rule into HOLD, and because any HOLD forces an overall HOLD, a single custom rule caps the entire batch at HOLD forever.

The {} is annotated as intentional (“no evidence-backed way to establish these yet”) — but the ICP builder still lets users add custom rules, so the UI offers a field that silently destroys the run.

03Setting allowed_titles holds every company at the company stageCritical

Company-stage validation is called with person_id=None, so candidate.title is None and the allowed_titles rule returns HOLD / TITLE_UNKNOWN — which forces overall HOLD before any person has been looked for. A title requirement is a normal thing to put in an ICP, and it poisons company qualification.

The order compounds it: person discovery runs after this gate, and a HOLD company still consumes enrichment and people-discovery calls without ever being able to reach ACCEPTED at that point.

04company_type is a hard rule that evidence can almost never satisfyCritical

All discovery evidence is written at ConfidenceLevel.UNKNOWN. An UNKNOWN record only becomes usable through the SUPPORTED_STRUCTURED allowlist, which covers exactly three fields — industry, country, employee_range — from exactly one provider. company_type is excluded by design, yet it is both a critical field and a hard rule.

So an ICP saying “D2C brands” HOLDs on COMPANY_TYPE_UNKNOWN unless two independent providers happen to agree — and in a single-provider deployment, two providers never agree. This is the same class of failure that employee_range already hit and had to be explicitly fixed in Phase 11.

05Compound ICPs are searched as OR, then repaired downstreamHigh

Explorium ANDs filters within one request, so the code deliberately splits “Healthcare” and “SaaS” into separate branches and merges the results — union, not intersection. The provider's own test asserts this on the wire: "SaaS" not in json.dumps(structured_filters).

The intersection is then reconstructed after the fact by cross-branch corroboration in validation. That reconstruction is genuinely clever and it works — but it means credits are spent on a diluted pool where most candidates match only one side, and recall depends on a company happening to surface in the right branches.

providers/explorium.py:1160–1234 · tests/test_explorium_provider.py:788 · services/hard_icp_validation.py:470+ Verified
06Title matching is exact-string onlyHigh

Titles are compared with clean_text(v).lower() == candidate — whitespace and case normalisation, nothing more. “VP Marketing” hard-FAILs a person titled “Vice President, Marketing”. There is no synonym table, no seniority ladder, no substring fallback.

The asymmetry is telling: industry got an entire bridging apparatus to soften exact matching, and exclusions use bidirectional substring matching via _text_matches. Titles got neither. Worse, attaching a person whose title cannot be matched actively downgrades a company that would have passed as company-only.

07No revenue, business-model, tech-stack, intent or exclusion filter reaches discoveryHigh

Only five filter keys are ever constructed: country_code, company_size, linkedin_category, naics_category, website_keywords. The filter catalog exposes revenue, technology, funding and hiring to the user, but those have no backing field and are projected into soft preferences — recorded, never sent anywhere.

Exclusions are the sharpest instance: hard.exclusions is never included in build_company_discovery_query, so Explorium is never told what to leave out. You pay to retrieve companies you have already declared unwanted, then filter them locally.

08Half the quality score measures data-richness, not ICP fitHigh

In the normal batch path, ICP-fit signals carry ~0.50 of the company-quality score and provenance/completeness signals carry the other ~0.50: discovery_provenance 0.20 (keyword-found scores 45 vs structured 90), evidence_strength 0.20, evidence_completeness 0.10 — the last being literally 100 × min(rows,10)/10, a row count.

Inside that, corroboration_score requires ≥2 distinct providers per field, so in a single-provider deployment it is structurally 0.0 for every company. company_quality_score is tie-break key #2 in ranking, so a perfectly-matched, keyword-discovered company sorts below a weaker structured one whenever final scores tie. It cannot change tier — but it buries within tier, which matters if anything consumes a top-N slice.

nlExtraction.ts says it outright in its own header: “Client-side heuristic NL → filter extraction. Not an LLM.” It is roughly 40 regexes that recognise seven industries (d2c, direct-to-consumer, fmcg, healthcare, ott, microdrama, saas) and 14 geographies. 02 Why a human finds 20 good companies and the system returns 50–100 useless ones

This is the question that matters, so here is the honest split. It is not one component. It is a chain where each stage is individually defensible and the composition is lossy — and the largest single term is simply that the providers are off.

The structural difference from a human researcher. A human searching “Healthcare SaaS” evaluates the intersection directly: they read a company's homepage and decide whether it is software sold to healthcare providers. This system cannot ask its provider for that intersection at all — Explorium ANDs filters within a request, so asking for both in one call would return near-nothing, and the codebase correctly splits into OR branches instead.

Everything downstream is then compensation for a question that was never askable. The bridging, cross-branch corroboration and free-text logic in hard_icp_validation.py are genuinely good engineering — but they are reconstructing an intersection from a union, which is strictly lossier than asking for it, and it costs credits to retrieve the companies that get discarded.

04 How Clay, Apollo and ZoomInfo actually do this

Only capabilities confirmed in current official documentation are stated below. Pricing, coverage claims and match rates are vendor marketing and are excluded.

Apollo — filter semantics

Documents AND logic across filters, OR logic within a filter: adding a filter narrows, adding a value inside one broadens. 65+ filters spanning firmographics, technographics (1,500+ technologies), buying intent, job postings and funding. Organization Search exposes revenue range, funding amount/date, HQ location and excluded locations.

This is precisely the semantic our Explorium layer cannot express — and the reason compound ICPs must be reconstructed downstream here.

Clay — waterfall + agentic research

Waterfall enrichment queries providers sequentially, cheapest first, stopping at the first valid result across 50+ databases — the explicit fallback architecture this codebase lacks. Claygent is an agentic web researcher that browses pages to answer questions “no database has a field for”, returning structured, cited output and ICP scores with rationale.

Notably, Clay's own guidance stresses cited output over “confident guesses” — the same anti-fabrication stance this codebase already enforces.

ZoomInfo — signals as first-class

Copilot builds buying groups from corporate websites, case studies and first-party CRM data. Account-level intent tracks topic-cluster research; documented signal types include job postings, funding rounds in the last 90 days, earnings-call and investor transcripts, partnerships and M&A.

Intent/signal data has no equivalent anywhere in this codebase.

Where you are genuinely ahead

None of the three documents a deterministic hard-rule gate that an LLM structurally cannot override, nor a per-field evidence-provenance model with explicit CONFLICT status and a HOLD state that is never terminal. Apollo and ZoomInfo return scored lists; auditability of why a company qualified is your differentiator.

05 What is genuinely working — and must not be changed

Do not weaken the hard-rule gate to raise PASS counts. _gated_result returns a structural result for FAIL/HOLD before provider.qualify() is ever reached, so an LLM cannot talk a failed candidate into GOOD_FIT. That guarantee is the backbone of the system. Every fix below removes a spurious HOLD by giving a rule real evidence — never by lowering the bar.

  • Deterministic rule engine — pure, no randomness, no LLM, no soft-preference leakage. Verified
  • Evidence provenance model — per-field status with genuine CONFLICT handling and a documented trusted-provider allowlist rather than blanket trust. Verified
  • Anti-hallucination on citations — a single fabricated evidence id fails the whole qualification with INVALID_EVIDENCE_IDS instead of being silently dropped. Verified
  • The “is vs merely mentions” prompt — explicitly instructs the model to distinguish a company that is the target type from an agency or consultancy that merely serves or mentions it. This is the exact discrimination the audit asked about, and it is already there. Verified
  • Compound-ICP repair chain — bridging, cross-branch corroboration and the free-text bridge each require full term coverage before widening, and each was root-caused from a real observed false positive. Verified
  • Company-first ordering — Phase 30 moved people discovery after validation and quality, so person-discovery credits are not spent on companies that will hard-fail. Verified
  • Mock/real isolation — when Explorium is configured, mock enrichment is dropped entirely, precisely because fake values were creating CONFLICT and permanent HOLDs. Verified
  • Test discipline — 1555 tests pass; regression tests encode prior live findings rather than restating the implementation. Verified

One correction to a claim you may have seen elsewhere. The gate is not missing from the batch path. There is no guard around create_lead_qualification in batch_orchestration.py:911, which looks alarming in isolation — but qualify_lead enforces the gate internally at services/llm_qualification.py:169–256, returning HARD_REJECTED / HARD_HOLD before any provider call. The documentation in .env.example is accurate. Verified

06 Target architecture — evolution, not rewrite

Every stage below already exists. Nothing here proposes replacing Explorium or Hermes: the evidence shows coverage is constrained by how they are queried and whether they are switched on, not by fundamental provider inadequacy.

The shape is right. Stage 4 is genuinely strong. The interventions are concentrated at the two ends — getting real data in (stages 1–3) and letting good companies out (stage 5) — which is why this is a wiring and rule-evidence problem rather than an architectural one.

08 Exact next action

One batch, one ICP, credentials rotated, spend capped

1. Rotate the three API keys currently sitting in backend/.env — they are committed in plaintext and should be treated as exposed regardless of what fixes follow.
