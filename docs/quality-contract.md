# Quality Contract

This is the canonical rulebook every future phase of Leads Agent must obey. It exists so that no
component — rule engine, scorer, LLM qualifier, adversarial reviewer, or learning loop — can drift from
the platform's core principle:

> **QUALITY > QUANTITY.** Never fabricate or guess critical lead information.

Two companion documents specialize parts of this contract:

- [Lead Decision Policy](lead-decision-policy.md) — states, transitions, accept/hold/reject/duplicate rules.
- [Evidence Policy](evidence-policy.md) — the provenance contract for critical fields.
- [Batch Contract](batch-contract.md) — rules for batch/bulk generation runs.

Nothing in this document is implemented yet (Phase 0 is documentation only). It defines the rules Phases
1–39 must implement against.

## 1. ICP Rules: Hard vs. Soft

Every ICP is normalized (Phase 2) into exactly two rule sets. They are never merged, and code must be able
to evaluate them independently.

### Hard rules

Hard rules are **deterministic, boolean, and mandatory**. A lead that fails one is disqualified,
regardless of how well it scores elsewhere.

- Geography (allowed countries/regions)
- Employee count bounds (min/max headcount)
- Allowed titles (for the decision-maker being evaluated)
- Industry requirements (allowed/required industries or industry codes)
- Explicit exclusions (named companies, domains, industries, or people to always reject)
- Any other requirement the ICP author marks as mandatory

**Rule:** Hard rules are evaluated by deterministic code (the Hard ICP Rule Engine, Phase 3) — never by an
LLM, a score, or an aggregate confidence value. See [Lead Decision Policy §4](lead-decision-policy.md) for
how this is enforced in the ACCEPT decision itself.

### Soft / commercial preferences

Soft preferences are **non-blocking** — they influence ranking and prioritization, never eligibility.

- Business model preferences (e.g. SaaS vs. services)
- Growth signals (hiring, funding, expansion)
- Commercial signals (tech stack, tooling spend, buying triggers)
- General prioritization preferences (e.g. "prefer larger accounts")

**Rule:** No combination of strong soft signals may substitute for a failed hard rule, and no single soft
signal may reject a lead on its own — see §3 (Anti-Fabrication) is not the mechanism for this; the
constraint lives in the decision policy: soft signals affect score and order, never PASS/FAIL.

### Immutability under feedback and LLM reasoning

Hard rules may only be changed by a deliberate, human-authored edit to the ICP (Phase 1/2 UI or config).
They must never be modified as a side effect of:

- Manager feedback (§6)
- LLM qualification or adversarial review output
- Scoring, ranking, or learning/optimization loops (Phase 25/26)

## 2. Anti-Fabrication Rules

The following fields are **critical fields**. A critical field must never be guessed, inferred without
disclosure, or generated by an LLM as if it were an observed fact:

- Company (identity/name)
- Person (identity/name)
- Current title
- Employee count
- Geography
- Industry
- LinkedIn identity (profile match)
- Company/person relationship (this person currently works at this company)
- Business model
- Evidence/source itself (an evidence record must not be invented to justify a value)

**Rule:** A critical field may only be used in a hard-rule check, shown as an accepted fact, or exported
once it has at least one evidence record meeting the [Evidence Policy](evidence-policy.md) minimum bar.

**Rule:** If a critical field cannot be evidenced, the system must not fill it with a plausible-looking
value. The only allowed outcomes are:

1. **HOLD** the lead and schedule further research, if more evidence is plausibly obtainable.
2. **REJECT** the lead, if the missing field is required by a hard rule and cannot be resolved.
3. Leave the field explicitly empty/unknown and lower confidence, if the field is not required by a hard
   rule and does not block acceptance.

Fabrication is never an allowed outcome, under any time or batch pressure (see [Batch
Contract](batch-contract.md)).

## 3. Manager Feedback Contract

Manager feedback is a structured signal, not a rule editor.

### Feedback categories

- `GOOD_FIT`
- `WEAK_FIT`
- `NOT_FIT`
- `HOLD`

### Reason codes

Every non-`GOOD_FIT` piece of feedback must carry a structured reason code, for example:

`WRONG_INDUSTRY`, `WRONG_GEOGRAPHY`, `WRONG_TITLE`, `COMPANY_TOO_SMALL`, `COMPANY_TOO_LARGE`,
`STALE_DATA`, `INSUFFICIENT_EVIDENCE`, `WRONG_BUSINESS_MODEL`, `POOR_TIMING`, `ALREADY_CUSTOMER`,
`DUPLICATE_OF_EXISTING`, `OTHER` (with free text).

This list is extensible in later phases but must stay a closed, structured set — not free text alone —
so it can drive ranking and discovery-strategy learning (Phases 25/26).

### What feedback may and may not do

**May influence:** ranking weights, discovery query strategy, commercial/soft scoring, prioritization of
similar future leads.

**Must never:** change a hard ICP rule's definition or threshold, reclassify a `REJECTED` (hard-rule
failure) lead as `ACCEPTED`, or bypass the [Evidence Policy](evidence-policy.md) minimum bar. If feedback
repeatedly signals that a hard rule itself is wrong (e.g. the geography rule is too narrow), that is a
signal for a human to edit the ICP deliberately — never an automatic rewrite.

## 4. Quality KPIs

These are the metrics that define success. Total lead count is explicitly **not** one of them.

- **Good-fit precision** — share of `ACCEPTED` leads later confirmed `GOOD_FIT` by manager feedback.
- **False-positive rate** — share of `ACCEPTED` leads later marked `NOT_FIT`.
- **Evidence completeness** — share of critical fields on `ACCEPTED`/`REVIEW` leads with valid evidence
  per the Evidence Policy.
- **Duplicate rate** — share of discovered leads later identified as `DUPLICATE`.
- **Human override rate** — share of system decisions (`ACCEPTED`/`HOLD`/`REJECTED`) changed by human
  review.

**Non-goal:** raw discovered/researched/accepted lead volume is a reporting metric (see [Batch
Contract](batch-contract.md)) only — it must never be optimized for directly, and no phase may trade
precision or evidence completeness for higher volume.

## Verification checklist for future changes

Any change touching ICP rules, scoring, LLM qualification, or feedback loops must be checked against:

1. Hard and soft rules remain clearly separated in code and data model.
2. `HOLD` remains reachable whenever evidence is insufficient or conflicting.
3. A hard-rule failure cannot result in `ACCEPTED`, regardless of score or LLM output.
4. Manager feedback cannot silently change a hard rule or an already-rejected hard-rule failure.
5. Every critical field on an `ACCEPTED` lead traces to at least one evidence record.
